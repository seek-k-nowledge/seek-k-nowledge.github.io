Research

Chaotic forecasting under irregular sampling

Continuous-Time Latent Dynamics for Long-Horizon Chaotic Forecasting under Irregular Sampling. Paul E. Harris IV, Independent Researcher. SynechismCore Technical Report, October 2026 (14 pages).

Architecture. SynechismV23 is a forecasting architecture I designed. It is a latent neural ODE: instead of updating on a fixed step, it learns a continuous rule for how its hidden state changes over time, so it can forecast to any point in time. It adds an elastic attractor term that keeps long forecasts bounded, golden-ratio sub-step scheduling for the integrator, and a laminar bypass that switches to a simpler linear update where the dynamics are smooth.

Question. When observations arrive at uneven times, does SynechismV23 keep chaotic forecasts accurate longer than three sequence-model baselines (a Transformer, an LSTM and a simplified Mamba-style state-space model) given the same timing information?

Setup. The Lorenz-63 system observed under four sampling regimes with the same nominal average rate: regular, jitter, half missing and exponential. Every model was trained for 200 epochs, over 5 seeds, and scored by valid prediction time (VPT) in Lyapunov times.

Results. SynechismV23 had the longest valid prediction time in all four regimes. The Transformer and Mamba-style baselines lost about 80% of their VPT between regular and exponential sampling. Against the LSTM, SynechismV23's lead under irregular sampling is small (4 of 5 seeds, 9–14%) and not yet statistically significant across seeds; more seeds are needed. The LSTM degrades least as timing becomes uneven; SynechismV23 ranks highest because it starts much higher.

Limits. One system (Lorenz-63); a separately trained model per sampling regime; the Transformer and Mamba-style baselines output a whole forecast block while SynechismV23 and the LSTM roll forward, so architecture and decoding are not yet separated; the Mamba-style model is a simplified implementation; component ablations are so far at 50 epochs only.

Next steps. Matched step-by-step baselines, a reference Mamba and a continuous-time Transformer, more seeds, full-budget ablations, a turbulence benchmark (Kuramoto–Sivashinsky), real robotics data, then arXiv and a 2027 workshop submission.

Project history. SynechismCore began as an exploratory study of forecasting under periodic correction. That study was discontinued, and this report supersedes it; only the irregular-sampling results above are claims of this work.
